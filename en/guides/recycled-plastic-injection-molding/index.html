Recycled plastic in injection-molded parts: how much to use and what to watch

Feasible percentages, effect on properties and color, traceability and how to communicate it to your customers.

7 min read · October 20, 2026 · Reviewed by SDH Plastics engineering

The short answer

There is no single percentage: it depends on the part's load, color and use. What does exist are clear rules. To make a recycled content claim in the U.S., the FTC expects the percentage by weight, and regrind reused in the same process does not count as recycled. Before you lock in a percentage, test parts with real lots of the recycled resin.

More and more buyers are handed recycled content targets by their end customer or their sustainability team. The practical question is how much recycled material the part can take without losing function, and how to back it up without creating a marketing problem.

This guide separates the types of recycled material, shows what changes in the part and explains which documentation supports a claim.

What is the difference between PCR, PIR and in-house regrind?

PCR (post-consumer recycled) comes from products a consumer already used: bottles, pails, containers. PIR (post-industrial or pre-consumer recycled) comes from manufacturing waste that was headed for disposal. In-house regrind is the runners and rejected parts a plant grinds and feeds back into the same part.

The FTC settles the point that causes the most confusion: scrap that industry normally reuses in the same process cannot be marketed as recycled content, because it was not headed for the waste stream.

How much recycled content can an injection-molded part take?

It depends on function. A dark part with no critical load and no food contact can reach high percentages. A structural part, a light-colored part or one with tight tolerances usually stays at lower percentages or needs a high-grade recyclate.

What happens to properties with recycled resin?

The main issue is variation. A virgin PP datasheet gives one typical value; a commercial recycled PP datasheet gives ranges. Vinmar's VERDANT RRPH2028Z, a recycled PP for injection molding, lists a melt flow rate of 20 to 28 g/10 min and tensile strength of 4,600 to 4,900 psi (32 to 34 MPa).

How does recycled content affect color and odor?

PCR tends toward a gray or yellow cast from reprocessing heat and residue from its first use, according to the U.S. Plastics Pact as cited by Waste Dive. In polyolefins, detergent fragrance can also linger, which devolatilization largely removes.

Brands use practical workarounds: dark colors, recycled content in hidden parts, and recycled material in the layer or component the user does not touch. If your part is white or light-colored, plan on a lower percentage or a premium-grade PCR.

Can I use recycled plastic in food-contact parts?

In the U.S., the FDA reviews each proposed food-contact use of recycled plastic case by case and issues an opinion on whether the recycling process yields material of suitable purity. In practice, molders use a recycled resin whose supplier already holds a favorable opinion for the intended use. In-house regrind of an already approved part is a different case, to review with your regulatory team.

Common mistake

Advertising a "part with 25% recycled material" when that 25% is the part's own ground runners. Under the FTC, that is not recycled content. Another frequent mistake: approving the part with one recyclate sample and then receiving lots with a different melt flow. Ask for the supplier's range and test at both the high and the low end.

How do you prove and communicate recycled content?

The FTC expects that, if the part is not 100% recycled, the claim clearly states the percentage by weight of the finished product. Splitting post-consumer from pre-consumer is optional, but if you do it you must be able to substantiate it.

Example: a 500 g (1.1 lb) part with 30% PCR contains 150 g of post-consumer material. Across 100,000 parts that is 15 metric tons, and each ton should trace back to a lot with a supplier certificate. A supported claim would read "30% post-consumer recycled content."

UL 2809 validates post-consumer, pre-consumer and closed-loop content claims measured by mass, and Europe adds EN 15343 for recycled plastics traceability. Inspection criteria for approving lots are in the guide to quality control for plastic parts, and choosing the base resin in the one on how to choose a resin.
