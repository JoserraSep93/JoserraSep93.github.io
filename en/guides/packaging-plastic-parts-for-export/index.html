Packaging plastic parts for export: fewer damages, lower freight

How to choose cartons, dividers and pallets, how many parts fit and how much freight per part changes.

7 min read · October 20, 2026 · Reviewed by SDH Plastics engineering

The short answer

With plastic parts, a truck usually fills up by space long before it reaches its weight limit. That is why packaging drives freight cost: in this guide's example, going from bulk-packed to nested parts cuts freight from 0.19 to 0.05 USD per part. The catch is that the carton has to survive the stack: a denser pack needs stronger board.

Why is freight for plastic parts paid by space, not by weight?

A 53-foot dry van holds 26 pallets of 48 × 40 inches on the floor and, per Hale Trailer, 42,000 to 45,000 pounds of cargo. A pallet of polypropylene parts weighs little: about 470 pounds (214 kg) in the example.

With 52 pallets double-stacked, the trailer is full by volume and at just over half its legal weight. Every inch of air inside the carton is freight you pay for.

How many parts fit per carton and per pallet?

Example assumptions (illustrative, not a quote):

  • Part: PP housing, 180 × 120 × 60 mm, 85 g.
  • Carton of 600 × 500 × 280 mm. Four cartons per tier on a 48 × 40 in pallet and four tiers: 16 cartons per pallet, 1.27 m (50 in) tall including the pallet.
  • Pallets double-stacked (2.54 m, 100 in), below the usable height of the trailer door.
  • Full truckload rate of 5,000 USD from the Bajío region to a warehouse in Texas (assumption; get a quote from your carrier).

On a part that sells for 1.15 USD, freight goes from 16% of the price (bulk) to 4% (nested). At 500,000 parts a year, the gap between A and C is about 71,000 USD in freight.

Layered packing also protects the part: parts do not knock against each other and arrive without scuffs. Bulk works when the part is rugged, low value and has no cosmetic surface.

How much weight does the bottom carton need to carry?

With double-stacked pallets, the bottom carton carries the 3 cartons in its column, the 4 cartons of the pallet above and its share of that pallet. In pattern C each carton weighs about 12.1 kg (27 lb), so the bottom one carries about 90 kg (198 lb).

Box compression strength is estimated with the McKee formula: 5.87 × ECT × sqrt(caliper × perimeter). That is a lab value. iPack publishes the real-world loss factors: 0.68 at 80% relative humidity, 0.60 after 30 days under load and 0.85 for well-aligned column stacking. Together they leave 35% of the original strength.

Double-wall board costs more. If it cost 1.50 USD more per carton (illustrative assumption), at 132 parts per carton that is 0.011 USD per part, against a freight saving of 0.14 USD per part compared with bulk. Run the numbers with your own prices.

Common mistake

Designing the carton around how many parts fit instead of how much weight it carries in a stack. In the example, the ECT 32 carton with nested parts has a 1.2 margin: in a humid warehouse or after a few weeks under load, the column collapses and the parts arrive deformed. The same happens when cartons overhang the pallet or are stacked interlocked instead of in columns.

Which pallet should you use for export, and what does customs require?

For the United States the standard is the 48 × 40 inch pallet (1,219 × 1,016 mm). For Europe, the 1,200 × 800 mm pallet. Choose the one used at the destination warehouse, not at origin.

If the pallet is solid wood, it must meet ISPM 15: heat treatment to at least 56 °C at the core for 30 minutes, or an approved fumigation, plus the official mark on two opposite sides. The U.S. requires it for wood packaging entering from Mexico (7 CFR 319.40-3).

What changes when the shipment goes by sea?

A 40-foot High Cube container measures 12.03 × 2.35 × 2.70 m inside, but its door is 2.58 m tall (DSV). That door limits the height of two stacked pallets. Loaded straight, 18 pallets of 48 × 40 fit per floor.

Ocean freight means more humidity and more days under load. Use the lower loss factors when sizing the carton, add a bag or desiccant if the part needs it and brace the load so it cannot shift.

How do you test packaging before the first shipment?

If you are moving production to Mexico, packaging is one of the steps in the guide to nearshoring plastic parts. To set who pays freight and carries the risk, see Incoterms for shipping from Mexico, and for duties, the guide to USMCA rules of origin.
