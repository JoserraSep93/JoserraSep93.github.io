Incoterms for shipping parts from Mexico

The 11 Incoterms 2020 in one table: who pays, who carries the risk and which fits each case.

8 min read · October 20, 2026 · Reviewed by SDH Plastics engineering

The short answer

An Incoterm settles two things: who pays for each leg of the shipment and at what point the risk of loss or damage moves from seller to buyer. For parts trucked from Mexico to the U.S., the most practical choices are usually FCA at the plant or DAP at the customer's warehouse. For ocean containers to South America, FCA at the terminal, or CPT and CIP to the destination port.

What is an Incoterm, and what does it leave out?

Incoterms are rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC). The current version is Incoterms 2020. Each one is written as three letters, a precise place and the version, for example: FCA Celaya, Guanajuato, Incoterms 2020. Without the place, the term is incomplete.

An Incoterm does not set the price, the payment terms, when ownership of the goods transfers, or what happens if there is a quality defect. Those belong in the contract or the purchase order.

Why do C terms pay to destination but shift risk at origin?

This is the most expensive misunderstanding. Under CPT, CIP, CFR and CIF the seller books and pays freight to destination, yet risk passes to the buyer as soon as the goods are handed to the carrier at origin. If the container is damaged at sea, the buyer files the insurance claim.

The D rules are the only ones where the seller carries the risk all the way to destination.

Which mistakes should you avoid?

Four common mistakes

FOB for containers. A container is handed over at the terminal days before it is loaded on the ship. Under FOB, the seller still carries the risk during that gap. The ICC points to FCA as the better fit for containerized cargo.

EXW for exports. Under EXW the foreign buyer must clear exports in Mexico, which in practice it cannot do without a Mexican exporter. FCA at the plant achieves the same thing with the seller handling export clearance.

Assuming CIF means you are well insured. CIF only requires minimum cover (Clauses C). For valuable parts, ask for CIP or a separate all-risk policy.

DDP into the U.S. without checking what it involves. Under DDP the seller is the importer of record with U.S. Customs. A non-resident company can be one, with a resident agent, an assigned importer number and a customs bond, and a June 2026 executive order tightens those requirements.

Which documents travel with an export shipment?

The Incoterm decides who handles each document, but the basic set is nearly the same on any route:

  • Commercial invoice showing the Incoterm, place and version exactly as written on the purchase order.
  • Packing list with parts per carton, cartons per pallet, weights and dimensions.
  • Export pedimento, filed by the customs broker in Mexico.
  • Transport document: carta porte for trucking in Mexico, bill of lading for ocean, air waybill for air.
  • Certification or certificate of origin if the destination grants preferential duty.
  • Insurance policy or certificate when the term is CIP or CIF, or when either party insures on its own.

If the invoice shows one Incoterm and the purchase order another, the argument starts right when something gets damaged. Check that they match before the goods ship.
