Injection mold life and maintenance: how to avoid downtime

What wears a mold, how often to service it and what downtime costs compared with preventive maintenance.

6 min read · October 20, 2026 · Reviewed by SDH Plastics engineering

The short answer

A well-kept steel mold lasts from hundreds of thousands to over a million cycles. What shortens its life is missed cleaning, lubrication and inspection. Schedule maintenance by cycles rather than by the calendar: a light service every 10,000 to 25,000 shots, preventive maintenance at 50,000 and a major overhaul around 300,000. In this guide's example, unplanned downtime costs about 20 times the preventive service that would have prevented it.

How many cycles does an injection mold last?

It depends on the steel and how the tool is run. The SPI classification that buyers and toolmakers use sets expected life at the design stage: a Class 101 mold is built for over a million cycles and a Class 104 mold for under 100,000. The full table is in how much an injection mold costs.

50,000 to 100,000shots of typical life for a P20 steel mold (Fictiv)
500,000 to 1 millionshots for a hardened H13 steel mold (Fictiv)
1 million or moreshots for an S136 stainless mold (Fictiv)

Those figures assume proper use. A poorly maintained Class 101 tool can fail long before a well-kept Class 103 tool. If you are choosing the mold material, see aluminum vs. steel molds.

Fictiv lists the most common causes. Each one leaves a sign on the part before it turns into downtime, and that is the payoff of inspection: the mold gives you a warning.

How often should an injection mold be maintained?

By cycle count. A mold running three shifts wears much faster than one running a week a month, so the calendar is misleading. Steve Johnson, a mold maintenance specialist quoted by MoldMaking Technology, frames it this way: a mold's "time" is measured in cycles or parts produced.

Reference intervals from the MaintainX checklist. Tune them with data: if the resin is glass-filled or the tool has many slides, shorten them. If three preventive services in a row show no wear, they can be extended.

What does downtime cost compared with preventive maintenance?

Preventive work happens between runs, with spare parts on hand and no lost press time. A breakdown arrives mid-production, with the press stopped, the order late and the repair shop charging rush rates.

Assumptions (illustrative, not a quote): 4-cavity mold on a 350-ton press costing $55 per hour. Preventive service at 50,000 shots: 6 technician hours at $30 ($180) plus $250 in springs and seals. Breakdown: an ejector pin breaks and scratches the cavity; the press sits idle 48 hours and the cavity is weld-repaired and polished at an outside shop.

What does a poorly cooled mold cost?

Not every cost shows up as downtime. MoldMaking Technology estimates that cooling takes about 80% of the molding cycle. When scale builds up in the water lines, the cycle creeps longer and nobody notices.

2 slonger cycle from fouled water lines (assumption)
278 hof extra press time over 500,000 shots
$15,300of extra cost at $55 per press hour

That is why the preventive service compares water flow against the mold's baseline. It is one of the cheapest checks and one of the biggest savers. It also moves the price per part.

Common mistake

Storing the mold without cleaning it or drying the water lines. Moisture rusts the cavity and channels, and the tool comes out of storage with pitting that must be polished before production. When pulling a mold: clean, blow the lines dry, apply rust preventive, log what you found and store it covered.

What records should a mold have?

  • A cycle counter updated every run.
  • Maintenance history: date, cycles, what was found and what was replaced.
  • Critical spares on hand: ejector pins, springs, seals, gate inserts.
  • Critical dimensions measured at every deep preventive, to see the trend.
  • A process sheet with the approved settings.

Fictiv recommends logging every service. With that history you can order a replacement tool before the current one fails. If you change suppliers, the history travels with the mold: see how to transfer a mold to a plant in Mexico.
