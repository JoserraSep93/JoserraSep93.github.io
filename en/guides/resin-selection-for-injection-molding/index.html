How to choose a resin for an injection-molded part: PP, HDPE, ABS, PC and nylon

A table to decide by strength, temperature, chemicals, finish and cost, with use cases.

8 min read · October 20, 2026 · Reviewed by SDH Plastics engineering

The short answer

Decide in this order: service temperature, impact, chemicals and moisture, appearance and, last, cost. PP and HDPE cover most general-purpose parts at the lowest cost. ABS gives good stiffness and finish for indoor housings. PC is for clarity, high impact and heat. Nylon 66 handles more heat and load, but it absorbs moisture and loses stiffness as it does.

If your question is only PP vs. HDPE, we covered it in the guide on polypropylene vs. HDPE. This guide widens the field to the five resins buyers quote most often in injection molding and compares them with numbers from manufacturer datasheets.

The values are typical of one specific grade in each family. Another grade of the same resin can differ by 20 or 30%, so use them to compare and confirm against the datasheet of the grade you will actually buy.

Which properties should I compare to choose a resin?

Five questions settle most cases: how stiff the part must be, how hot it runs, what impacts it takes, which chemicals or moisture it lives with, and what it costs per part. The table puts each resin's data in one place.

Which resin is the stiffest?

Of the five, dry nylon 66 and ABS. An ABS wall deflects about half as much as the same PP wall under the same load, because its modulus is nearly double. That is why ABS is common in housings that take screws and must not bow.

Which resin handles the most heat?

Nylon 66, followed by PC. Heat deflection temperature tells you when the part starts to deform under a light load. If your part sits near a motor or a heating element, or goes through hot water, this number narrows the list quickly.

Note the footnote: at 1.8 MPa, unreinforced nylon falls to 70 °C, below ABS. For parts that carry load while hot, the usual answer is glass-filled nylon. We explain it in the guide to glass-filled plastics.

Which resin takes impacts best?

PC, at 65 kJ/m² ISO Izod at 23 °C, and ABS, at 210 J/m ASTM Izod. Both are amorphous resins that absorb energy before they break. PP homopolymer, at 32 J/m, is the most brittle of the group; if you need PP with impact strength, the copolymer grade is the way out.

210 J/mABS Izod impact at 23 °C, 6.5 times PP homopolymer
5.5 → 12kJ/m² for dry vs. conditioned nylon 66: moisture makes it tougher

Which resin absorbs moisture, and why does it matter?

Nylon. The Zytel 101L datasheet lists 2.6% water at equilibrium with 50% humidity and 8.5% at saturation. As it absorbs water, nylon gets tougher but loses more than half its stiffness and grows slightly in size.

3,100 → 1,400 MPatensile modulus of dry vs. conditioned nylon 66: a 55% loss in stiffness from ambient humidityDuPont, Zytel 101L NC010 datasheet

PP and HDPE absorb almost no water, and PC takes up 0.12% under the same conditions. If the part needs stable dimensions in a humid environment, those three come first. The tolerances each resin can hold are covered in the guide to injection molding tolerances.

Which resin resists chemicals best?

PP and HDPE resist non-oxidizing acids and bases, detergents and most solvents at room temperature well. They are the default choice for containers, trays and parts that get washed with chemicals.

PC has specific weak spots. Covestro, its manufacturer, warns that alkalis accelerate its hydrolysis, that ammonia and low-molecular-weight amines are particularly aggressive when water is present, and that Makrolon does not resist gasoline or diesel. A PC part with molded-in stress can also crack on contact with certain chemicals, and in opaque colors the crack is hard to see.

ABS is sensitive to sunlight: Hubs describes it as prone to cracking under UV light and heat. For outdoor use it needs protection or a different resin.

Common mistake

Choosing nylon from the dry datasheet value. A part designed around 2,800 MPa of stiffness actually works at about 1,200 MPa after a few weeks in a normal environment. Ask your engineer for conditioned values and design with them. The same care applies on the floor: nylon and PC are dried before molding, or the part comes out brittle.

Which resin costs the least per part?

PP and HDPE are the cheapest per pound and also the lightest. Cost per part depends on price per pound and part weight, and weight depends on density. For a part with a volume of 200 cm³:

Hubs ranks relative cost this way: PP and HDPE at the lowest tier, ABS one tier up, nylon 6 two tiers up and PC at the top. Resin prices move every month, so when two candidates are close, ask for a quote on both. The full price formula is in the guide to how price per part is calculated.

How do I pick the right resin step by step?

If two candidates survive the tree, quote both and test samples under real service conditions: temperature, chemical and impact. For a fast quote, check what you need to get a part quoted and include those conditions.
