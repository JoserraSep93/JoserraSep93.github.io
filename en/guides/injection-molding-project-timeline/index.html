Injection molding project timeline: from idea to first production

The stages, how long each takes, which decisions stretch them and how to cut weeks safely.

7 min read · October 20, 2026 · Reviewed by SDH Plastics engineering

The short answer

For a new part with a steel mold, plan on 15 to 30 weeks, including one round of corrections, from quote request to approval of first production. Building the mold, 8 to 10 weeks in a typical case, is less than half of the schedule. What stretches projects most are late design changes, extra sampling rounds and shipping the mold internationally.

The most common planning mistake is asking only how long the mold takes. An injection molding project has seven stages before series production, and several of them depend on your decisions, not the toolmaker's. This guide gives published ranges for each stage so you can build a realistic schedule.

How long does an injection molding project take from idea to first production?

15 to 30 wkfrom quote to production approval, with one correction round (sum of the ranges below)
≈3 monthsfrom design release to first article on a U.S. production mold (MIT)
8 to 10 wkto build a typical mold (Rosti)
+6 wkif the mold ships by ocean from Asia, with customs at both ends

The range is wide because the project changes a lot with mold complexity, cavity count and how quickly decisions get made. A single-cavity cap sits at the low end. A housing with side actions, texture and critical tolerances sits at the high end.

What are the stages and how long does each one take?

The durations below are planning ranges taken from the manufacturers published in the sources. They are industry references, not a commitment from any supplier.

Which stage takes up most of the schedule?

Mold build is the longest stage, but it is not the only one that matters. Using the midpoint of each range, the mold takes 44% of the schedule. The other 56% sits in stages where your responses count: quoting, design review, approvals and corrections.

Common mistake

Planning the launch around mold build time as if it were the whole project. If you hear "the mold is 10 weeks out" and set the sales date at 12, there is no room for quoting, design approval, the T1 trial or a single correction round. Ask for the full schedule by stage, with who decides at each one.

Which decisions stretch an injection molding project?

  • Design changes after steel is cut. Each change goes back through tooling, trial and measurement. A design for manufacturing review before releasing the mold prevents most of them.
  • Samples waiting on feedback. T1 samples sitting in approval stop the whole project. Define who approves and within how many days.
  • A complex mold. Side actions, lifters, multiple cavities or a hot runner add weeks of build and tuning.
  • A specialty resin. An off-catalog grade or color can carry its own lead time.
  • Undefined acceptance criteria. If nobody agreed on which dimensions are critical and how they are measured, approval turns into a negotiation.

How do you cut weeks without adding risk?

  • Design review before the final quote. It is the stage where a change costs hours, not weeks.
  • Parallel work. While the mold is being built, define packaging, inspection criteria and the PPAP or first article package, and order the resin.
  • Approvals with a date. One person responsible for approving the mold design and the samples, with an agreed turnaround.
  • Steel-safe design. Doubtful dimensions placed on the side that keeps metal get corrected by removing steel, which is faster than adding it.
  • A bridge tool. If the launch cannot wait, an aluminum mold can deliver parts sooner; compare the options in aluminum vs. steel molds.
  • An existing mold. If you already own a mold, the schedule is different: see how to transfer a mold to Mexico.

What lead times do quick-turn injection molding services quote?

Xometry quotes T1 samples from 14 business days on its standard service and from 5 on quick-turn. Fictiv cites 12 business days for a simple single-cavity part and says a traditional supplier takes about twice as long. MIT Orbit places these services at 6 to 8 weeks, versus about 3 months for traditional U.S. production tooling.

Those are real lead times for simple molds with few cavities and no actions. For a production part with multiple cavities, texture or tight tolerances, the ranges in this guide are the more prudent reference.
