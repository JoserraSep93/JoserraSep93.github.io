    How it is calculated

    This is the rule of thumb the industry uses to size a press before a mold filling analysis is available.

    1. Total area. We multiply the projected area of one part by the number of cavities and add 10% for runners and sprue.
    2. Material factor. Each resin needs a certain force per cm² of area. We use a typical low to high range (table below).
    3. Adjustments. We multiply by 1.25 for thin walls or 1.5 for very thin walls, and by 1.2 for glass-filled material.
    4. Safety margin. We add 10% to the high value and round to whole tonnes.
    Low = total area × low factor × adjustments High = total area × high factor × adjustments × 1.10

    Example: polypropylene box, 30 × 20 cm, 2 cavities, normal wall, unfilled

    Area of one part30 × 20 = 600 cm²
    Total area600 × 2 × 1.10 = 1,320 cm²
    PP factor1.5 to 2.5 tons/in² = 0.2109 to 0.3515 t/cm²
    Low1,320 × 0.2109 = 278 t
    High1,320 × 0.3515 × 1.10 = 510 t

    Result: ≈ 278 – 510 t. At SDH, 3 of 17 presses cover it: the 650, 950 and 1,300 t machines.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is clamp force in injection molding?
    It is the force the press uses to keep the mold closed while plastic is injected under pressure. If it is lower than the force of the plastic pushing back, the mold opens slightly and the part comes out with flash. It is measured in tonnes.
    What is projected area?
    It is the part's shadow seen along the direction the mold opens. You add up the area of every cavity and the runners, because the melt pressure acts on that whole surface.
    How accurate is this calculation?
    It tells you which press range your part falls into. The actual value depends on flow length, wall thickness, gate, temperature and mold design. It is confirmed by plant experience or a mold filling analysis.
    Should I use a press with far more tonnage than needed?
    No. Clamping with too much force can crush the vents and wear the mold parting line, and a larger press costs more per hour. The usual choice is the press that covers the high estimate with a reasonable margin.
